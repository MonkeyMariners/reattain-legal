Privacy Policy

Last updated · 20 September 2026

REATTAIN counts the short videos you scroll past, lets you block distracting apps and websites for a focus session you set yourself, and makes you earn an early exit by completing a challenge. This policy explains, in plain terms, what the app does and does not do with your information. It is written to match how the app actually behaves.

The short version. REATTAIN runs on your phone. Your sessions, reel counts, challenge results and daily totals are stored on the device. To make blocking and counting work, the app reads small, specific parts of what is on screen — which app is in front, whether a feed scrolled, the address in your browser's address bar, and the channel name on a YouTube watch page. None of that is stored or sent anywhere. If you turn on cloud backup, only counts and outcomes are copied to your private account. If you turn on friends, a short profile becomes visible to people you add.

What stays on your device

The app's own database lives on your phone and is the source of truth. It holds your sessions, your challenge attempts, your reel and short-video counts, your emergency contacts, your lock profiles, your blocked websites, your allowed YouTube channels and your daily statistics. This information is not sent anywhere except as described under "Cloud backup" and "Friends and focus rooms" below.

The Accessibility Service — what it reads, and why

REATTAIN uses Android's Accessibility Service to block apps, block websites and count short videos. This use is not related to assisting users with disabilities. It is optional, you turn it on yourself in your phone's Accessibility settings, and you can turn it off at any time.

This is the most powerful permission Android grants, so here is precisely what the app does with it. The service is configured to be able to read window content, because three of the features below are impossible without it.

What it reads

  • Which app is in the foreground — the package name. This is how a blocked app is noticed during a session.
  • That a feed scrolled, and by how much — a number, not content. This is how a reel swipe is counted on Instagram, TikTok, Facebook and X.
  • On YouTube and Snapchat, the text visible on the current short video — the creator's @handle and the caption. These apps report no usable scroll, so the only way to tell one short from the next is to read what identifies the one on screen. That text is immediately converted into a single number (a fingerprint) and discarded. The text itself is never stored, never logged and never leaves the device. Only the number is compared, and only to the previous number.
  • On YouTube, the channel name on a watch page — only when you have switched on Study Mode, and only to check it against the list of channels you allowed.
  • In your browser, the address shown in the address bar — only when you have added blocked websites or switched on the adult-site filter. The app keeps only the host (for example example.com); the rest of the address — the page, the path, anything you searched for — is discarded before any comparison is made, so the app never holds the part of a web address that identifies a page.

What it never reads

  • Your messages, in any app.
  • Passwords, PINs, or anything you type.
  • The contents of your notifications.
  • Photos, files, or anything on screen beyond the specific items listed above.

What happens to it

Nothing the service reads is stored, logged, or transmitted. The only things that outlive the moment are numbers: a count of reels per app per day, and the counts behind your daily statistics. Those stay on your device unless you switch on cloud backup or friends.

Uninstall protection (optional, off by default)

If you switch this on, then during a focus session or while your daily reel limit is spent, the app sends you back to your home screen if the system uninstaller, an app store's page for REATTAIN, or the Settings "App info" screen for REATTAIN comes to the front. This reads only the name of the window in front — never its contents — and acts only on those screens. It never blocks Settings generally, it is never active outside those two situations, and a session always ends on its own. It is a deterrent against an impulse, not a lock: you can turn it off, uninstall in safe mode, or simply switch the Accessibility Service off.

Screen time

If you grant usage access, the app shows you per-app screen time. These figures come from Android's own records through UsageStatsManager — Android has already measured them; the app only reads them, on demand, for a screen you opened. They are not stored by the app and are never transmitted.

Cloud backup (optional, Firebase)

So your progress survives a lost or replaced phone, the app can back up a limited copy of your history to Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore) under an account that is yours alone.

  • You start anonymous. On first launch the app signs in with Firebase Anonymous Authentication, which creates a random identifier with no name, email or phone number attached.
  • You can sign in with Google to keep your history across devices. If you do, your Google account's name and email address become associated with your data, and your existing anonymous history is linked to that account rather than abandoned.
  • Only you can read your data. Security rules restrict every document to the account that created it.

Friends and focus rooms (optional, off by default)

These are the only features where another person can see anything about you, and nothing happens until you choose to publish. They require signing in with Google, because an anonymous identity disappears when the app is reinstalled.

  • What becomes visible: your display name, your chosen avatar, today's total reel count, and your current focus streak. Nothing else.
  • What never becomes visible: your session history, which apps you block, your per-app breakdown, your screen time, your blocked websites, your challenge answers, or anything else in the app.
  • Who can see it: people you add as friends, and — while you are in a focus room — the other people in that room, who see your first name, avatar and minutes focused.
  • Turning it off: removing your profile from the Friends screen deletes it, and everything stops. Leaving a room removes you from it.

Sound and speech

Focus sounds are generated on your device or played from files included in the app. Nothing is streamed and nothing is recorded — the microphone is never used. The guided tour can read itself aloud using your phone's built-in text-to-speech; the text spoken is the text on screen, and the app does not send it anywhere. Depending on your phone's settings, your device's speech engine may itself process text; that is governed by your phone manufacturer's and speech engine's own privacy policy.

Crash reports and analytics

The app uses Firebase Crashlytics and Analytics to understand crashes and general usage (for example, how many sessions are completed), so we can fix problems and improve the app. These reports are aggregated and are not tied to your name or identity. They never include the contents of your sessions, your challenge answers, your contacts, your browsing, or camera data.

What this app never does

  • Read your messages, your passwords, or the contents of your notifications.
  • Record or upload anything the camera or microphone could capture.
  • Track your location.
  • Keep or transmit your browsing history.
  • Sell or share your data with advertisers or data brokers.
  • Stop you removing the app, except during a session you started with uninstall protection switched on — and never in a way you cannot undo.

Keeping and deleting your data

Data on your device is removed when you uninstall the app or clear its storage. Backed-up data is kept in your account so your history can be restored. To have your backed-up data and any published profile deleted, contact us at reattain@gmail.com and we will remove it. You can delete your published profile yourself at any time from the Friends screen.
